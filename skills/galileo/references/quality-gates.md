# Evidence sufficiency and delivery checks

Read the relevant sections for a full manuscript, thesis, rendered presentation or a tracked revision. A bounded language edit or content-only outline needs only the checks affected by that request; do not turn these checks into another setup questionnaire or mandatory ledger.

## 1. Decide what the materials can support

Before factual drafting, identify the intended document and its central claims. Reuse supplied answers and inspect authorized material first. Adapt essential information to the work: an empirical study needs its question, methods and actual results; an implementation thesis needs the problem, author's contribution, implemented approach and evaluation; a proposal needs planned methods rather than invented completed results. A reconstruction is a valid requested product, but must not silently replace a report of work actually performed.

For essential information distinguish:

- **Available and inspected:** a consulted source with a locator, or information explicitly supplied by the user. Preserve the distinction between an author-reported result and an independently reproduced result.
- **Available but not inspected:** inspect it within the authorized scope before claiming it is missing.
- **Present but excluded:** respect the source restriction and record its consequence. Do not infer the contents of an unread excluded source; describe information as unavailable within the permitted corpus rather than absent from the archive.
- **Missing or ambiguous:** ask the smallest useful question or request a specific authorized source. A screenshot or signature cannot establish execution, an algorithm, or a measurement that it does not show.

Ask up to three focused questions at a time and wait for answers before dependent factual work. Continue only work that does not depend on the gaps; silence is not an answer. If the user intentionally restricts sources or chooses a partial draft, explain the consequence once and respect that choice. A restriction does not authorize a silent change from the requested thesis to a different document. For a requested final account of completed research, unresolved essentials block final readiness: ask for the missing information and retain a provisional draft rather than completing it with plausible prose. Deliver a reconstruction or narrower final product only when the user has selected that scope. Do not block a planning document because results do not yet exist.

Do not use hypotheses, generic best practices, invented examples or repeated caveats to fill holes in factual methods or results. Clearly requested proposals and illustrative examples remain permissible with honest labels and source support where needed; they do not establish what happened in the user's project.

Keep a compact sufficiency summary in the existing project note/state when a run is tracked: intended product, essential available facts, remaining gaps and their source status, affected claims, next question/action. No new form or configuration step is required from the user.

### Resolve literature gaps actively

For a substantive scientific manuscript or thesis, map the outline's background, definitions, rationale and interpretations to the literature actually consulted. Reuse verified sources and search records when they cover the task; a supplied bibliography or search-result snippet alone does not establish coverage or support. Identify material gaps before writing the dependent claims and revisit them during QA.

For an external-knowledge gap, perform a targeted search using available authorized academic databases, search tools or official primary technical documentation appropriate to the discipline. Read the relevant primary source content, verify the claim and metadata, and record query/date, source locator, access/full-text limits and what it supports. Use literature-review/citation-management when installed and relevant; a focused background search need not become a systematic review. Keep historical implementation sources distinct from current recommendations. Ask a scoping question only when it materially changes the search; do not ask the user to supply general literature that available tools can locate.

For a project-specific gap—what was implemented, sample selection, instrument version, actual methods, results or test outcomes—ask the author or inspect an authorized project source. General literature cannot establish those facts. If both kinds of gap exist, search the public background while awaiting the author's answer, without exposing confidential details in queries. Do not contact third parties or activate paid services automatically.

If search or relevant full text is unavailable or forbidden, state the concrete limitation and leave dependent claims/references unverified and final readiness pending. Offer a useful permitted source/MCP route where appropriate; do not fabricate citations, claim a search occurred, infer unseen results from abstracts, or use polished wording to bypass the gap. A bounded edit or source-only outline does not require an unrelated new literature search.

### Develop and verify sections progressively

For a full manuscript/thesis, use an agreed outline and a section-sized drafting/check/correction loop. Resolve the current section’s literature and project-information gaps before dependent prose. Reuse the user’s choice of interactive checkpoints or autonomous progression; essential factual questions still pause dependent work in either mode. Start with a provisional introduction where useful, but finalize claims about findings, abstract and conclusions after methods/results or implementation/evaluation are established. Track status in existing notes/state. Reassess affected earlier sections when new information arrives; perform a whole-document consistency pass and final-version QA after assembly. A section pass alone cannot establish final readiness. See [drafting guidance](../../research-drafting/SKILL.md#section-by-section-development).

## 2. Separate evidence review from usefulness to the reader

**Evidence and content review:** check central claims against permitted sources; distinguish observations, inferences, author-reported historical results, actual reproduction and proposed checks. Reconcile title, abstract, sections and conclusion with the intended document. Assets from unknown or mixed versions support a statement about the supplied materials, not necessarily one historical release. Do not use demonstration dates as project chronology. Keep unresolved source conflicts explicit.

Treat unsupported central claims, invented project facts/results/references, unresolved methods or outcome contradictions, and missing evidence needed for the conclusion as blocking findings. Check whether each central citation supports the exact claim, not merely whether the source exists. Remove an unsupported peripheral claim or narrow a conclusion only when meaning and the requested scope remain valid; a central gap requires evidence or an author decision, not a cosmetic rewrite. For a full scientific final-delivery request, have a reviewer distinct from the writer verify the central evidence/method findings when the host permits it. If unavailable, keep that independent check pending and disclose the limit rather than claiming rigorous final verification.

**Editorial and visual review:** check whether the reader can understand the problem, terminology, approach and findings in a useful order. Introduce unfamiliar domain objects before detailed diagrams, with a source-grounded example where helpful. Put production logs and lengthy reconstruction commentary in project provenance or an appendix unless they are the scientific subject; retain qualifications that affect interpretation in the text. Avoid repeating the same missing-evidence warning in every paragraph.

**Terminology review:** preserve the document language while using the discipline's established terms and the user's terminology preference. Do not translate technical vocabulary merely to make every word local. In a non-English computing thesis, established English terms such as `framework`, `dependency injection`, `rollback` or `connection pool` may be clearer than an invented translation. Accepted local equivalents are not automatically errors; prefer the stated convention consistently and briefly explain unfamiliar concepts in the reader's language. Preserve exact identifiers, API/class names, commands, units, instrument names and source titles; `BeanFactory` is not a label to translate.

For a full project with recurring or ambiguous technical terms, keep a compact term list in existing notes: preferred term, accepted alias/first-use explanation, concept and consulted domain source or user convention. Use supplied documentation and authorized primary technical sources when ambiguity matters; do not infer that all terms in one language are canonical. Check title, headings, captions, tables, notes and slides against the same choices. Separate awkward phrasing from a translation that changes the technical meaning. Terminology edits must preserve claims and qualifiers; an uncertain semantic change returns to a domain/content reviewer. A small text edit needs this check, not a new glossary artifact or another agent by default.

For documents, inspect actual pages for short continuations isolated before chapter breaks, excessive whitespace caused by misplaced figures/tables, readable captions and figures, index/cross-reference accuracy and the chosen page size/template. A deliberate figure page or institutional blank page can be justified; do not impose a universal whitespace threshold or shrink text merely to fill a page.

For slides, inspect at the intended presentation size. Ask whether the audience can read the details needed for the message, not merely whether text fits the canvas. Use faithful crops, annotations or a simpler conceptual view for the main talk; keep full technical detail in backup slides when appropriate. Do not invent examples, remove inconvenient evidence, alter source figures deceptively or add slides beyond the requested count without resolving the scope. Distinguish estimated duration from a timed rehearsal.

Record evidence/content and editorial/visual findings separately in existing review records. More agents or a successful export are not proof that either check passed.

Within editorial review, record terminology issues separately from grammar when the distinction affects meaning or disciplinary usage. A language-only pass does not establish terminology accuracy.

## 3. Close checks against the delivered revision

When a run tracks versions, each material check identifies:

- check kind and status: performed, pending, failed, not applicable or superseded;
- artifact path and version/hash, plus source-to-export linkage where relevant;
- reviewer or tool, actual independent/sequential/self-review context;
- scope and evidence: sections, pages/slides, render size or inspection method, result/log locator;
- unresolved findings and the correction or missing input needed.

After an edit, invalidate affected checks rather than copying a previous pass to the new file. Reuse an unchanged page/slide inspection only with documented identity and unchanged relevant source/renderer conditions; label that as reused evidence. Otherwise recheck affected content and layout, including downstream pagination, references, notes and exports. Build the requested PDF from the final editable revision and reconcile count/order/content.

If a distinct verifier is unavailable, continue authorized corrections and report a coordinator/self-review fallback. Keep independent review pending where it was required by the selected workflow or requested by the user; do not invent a second reviewer, self-close scientific reviewer issues, or claim the earlier independent pass covers the final changed revision. A useful qualified handoff is possible without automatic acceptance or a fabricated verified status.

Before final readiness, verify that essential questions are resolved, required literature gaps are covered by consulted sources, no blocking content/terminology/visual findings remain, and required checks cover the delivered revision. A passed checklist, successful export, number of agents or fluent text cannot substitute for this assessment. At a review/cost/capability limit, hand off the useful draft and open findings; never turn the limit into automatic acceptance. Do not promise absolute correctness or certify scientific validity from AI review.

At delivery distinguish **files produced**, **checks performed on this revision**, **checks still pending** and **human approval**. Inspecting every page does not by itself establish good design or scientific merit. Mention limits affecting the requested use, with the concrete question/source/check needed next; ordinary reversible choices do not need an extra approval stop.
