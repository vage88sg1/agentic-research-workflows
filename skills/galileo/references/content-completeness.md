# Objectives, chapter completeness and bibliography coverage

Use for full scientific manuscripts/theses and substantive reviews or conversions. Reuse existing project notes, source/claim records and outline; do not ask the user to fill in a new template. A bounded edit checks only the affected meaning/citations. This reference supplements [evidence and final-revision gates](quality-gates.md).

## Agree the product and identify the contribution

Confirm the requested document and central question before expanding a draft. For work already performed, distinguish the author's actual contribution, pre-existing infrastructure/data, collaborators' work and new analysis by the writing assistant. Ask only about unresolved distinctions that affect attribution. For a proposal, distinguish intended contributions from completed work. Do not invent novelty, authorship or a historical rationale from a plausible design advantage.

Reuse a compact objective map in the outline or existing notes:

| Objective/question | Relevant section and contribution | Claims and source locators | What evidence can establish | Coverage and next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Actual user-supplied objective | Intended answer and attributed work | Existing claim/source IDs or consulted passages | Documented description, author-reported outcome, reproduced result or explicit proposal | Supported / partial / blocked / outside the agreed scope; missing fact/search/check |

Evaluate substantive fulfillment, not the presence of a heading, screenshot or citation. A screen shows a represented function; a method signature identifies an interface. Neither establishes an algorithm, successful execution or achievement of an objective. An author's historical outcome can be reported as such without requiring an executable reproduction today; do not relabel it independently reproduced. A proposal need not contain completed results.

At each section checkpoint and whole-document review, assess central objectives against this map. A partial central answer blocks final completeness unless the user explicitly changes the intended scope. If title, objectives, contribution or central question change, reconsider the outline, requirements/methods, result interpretation, discussion and conclusion; update the evidence dependencies before editing isolated sentences. Preserve the earlier scope/version rather than silently rewriting it.

## Set a useful purpose for each major section

Before drafting a chapter/major section, record its question, necessary explanation, supporting material and dependencies in the existing outline. Match expectations to the discipline and genre rather than imposing a universal chapter list:

- Background/context should explain the problem, relevant concepts and why the question matters, using external sources for general knowledge and authorized project facts for the actual setting.
- Empirical methods should explain design, population/unit, selection, instruments, procedures and analysis sufficiently for the actual claims. Missing reporting differs from evidence of an invalid study; ask for what is genuinely necessary.
- An implementation section should explain actual rules, transformations, interfaces and the supported operational flow, with source/specification/code excerpts where needed. A list of class names or a proposed algorithm cannot stand in for the implemented algorithm.
- Results/evaluation should distinguish observed, author-reported and reproduced outcomes from future checks. Discussion should answer the objectives, interpret limitations and compare relevant alternatives with appropriate evidence.

For a central flow or concept that remains hard to follow, use a documented worked example when available. A clearly labeled synthetic teaching example may explain verified concepts; it cannot supply project-specific rules, measured benefits or missing outcomes. Distinguish a known historical decision/rationale from a new analysis of design consequences. Do not invent alternatives allegedly considered by the original team.

## Audit bibliography coverage and integration

A bibliography is not complete merely because it contains valid entries. Map substantive background, definitions, rationale, comparisons and interpretation to the sources actually consulted. Identify material topics/claims without adequate support and perform targeted searches before dependent drafting, following [active literature searches](quality-gates.md#resolve-literature-gaps-actively). Reuse already verified coverage; do not demand a new search for every paragraph.

Select source type for the assertion:

- Official specifications/API manuals explain mechanisms, interfaces and versioned behavior; they do not prove empirical benefits or the user's configuration.
- Original conceptual works and suitable scholarly synthesis explain theory, rationale or state of knowledge. A state-of-the-art comparison needs relevant alternatives, not only the manual of the selected product.
- Empirical studies support reported outcomes in their actual population/design/settings; they cannot establish benefits in the user's project without project evidence.
- Authorized project records or explicit author answers support what happened in the work. Keep these identifiable separately from public literature; an inventory of screenshots is not a replacement for conceptual sources.

In technical writing, manuals can be legitimate references. Do not impose a minimum number of papers, invent citations to make the list academic, or label a focused narrative search systematic. Choose appropriate primary and synthesis sources for the discipline and claim; a review supports a synthesis claim but does not authorize inventing unseen study details. Keep historical documentation distinct from current recommendations.

Check both directions: every citation resolves to the correct bibliography entry and supports the specific assertion; every included bibliography entry has an identifiable use or a justified separately labeled further-reading purpose. Verify metadata/identifiers, consulted version/edition/date where relevant, locators, citation style and links with available tools. Never invent a date/DOI for a manual or a missing source. Internal source IDs should resolve to accessible authorized material or clear access limits, with readable figures/appendices where useful. Keep production logs and procedural skill credits in provenance unless they are the research subject.

Record material uncovered topics, search/access limits and the next action in existing notes. Essential claim support still unavailable means dependent prose/final readiness remains pending. Metadata-only validation, a long reference list or fluent paraphrase cannot close this gap.

## Review from the reader's position

Reuse the domain reviewer for a reader-focused pass, in a separate context when supported/authorized; do not add another mandatory reviewer to every run. Provide the intended readership, current artifact and permitted sources, without the writer's desired verdict or previous first-pass reports. Ask what the manuscript itself establishes about the problem, domain objects, method/flow, attributed contribution and outcomes. Identify missing links, unsupported leaps, unclear terminology and whether the conclusion answers the objectives. The absence of a necessary explanation is a finding even when every present sentence is true.

In sequential/self-review, declare the limitation. Keep domain/evidence findings distinct from grammar and layout. Consolidate repetitive caveats and production commentary while preserving qualifications that change interpretation; do not fill space to hit a page count or delete inconvenient uncertainty. A section pass does not replace whole-document and final-version review.
